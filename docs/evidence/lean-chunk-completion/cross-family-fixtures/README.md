# Cross-family whole-chunk fixtures (W05, W07, W09, W10)

Validator-produced chunks from the pinned nearcore `44f7ae6cd7ef08bab604e20a473bf77e35d4c993`
(PV86). They were generated by `oracle/v3-successor` with five new opt-in scenarios (`src/xfam.rs`,
`NEAR_SUCCESSOR_SCENARIO=yield-congestion | curve-callbacks | genesis-tx | gaskey-target |
rollback-refund`). Each scenario targets shapes that no earlier fixture set covered:

1. yields under real congestion;
2. curve hosts called from callbacks and promise chains, and the remaining BLS-writer
   register/out-of-bounds inputs;
3. transaction-bearing genesis segments;
4. gas keys on the receiving shard;
5. failed initialisation, refunds and emitted receipts in one chunk, together with global contracts.

No signed source changed (`make pin-check` passes).

Every honest chunk was accepted by the cold nearcore validator. Every verdict below is compared
with that validator's verdict on the same bytes. Lean computes; nearcore's results are never
inputs.

**Result: no Lean/nearcore accept–reject disagreement in 33,182 compared verdict rows (25 runs,
5 families). No Lean change was needed.** The only non-matching rows are 9 code-blob mutants of
baseline traffic. On these, Lean returns the named out-of-domain verdict `e.wasm-α` ("contract
preparation pipelined before the in-chunk deploy that serves its code", the W08 thread race), and
nearcore's verdicts on equivalent mutants of the same chunk differ (see "Non-matching rows").
Deliberate-breakage controls show that each family detects a bug in its own semantics.

## Reproduce

```
bash oracle/scripts/link-nearcore.sh
CARGO_TARGET_DIR=/tmp/lean-global-oracle-target cargo build --release --manifest-path oracle/v3-successor/Cargo.toml
B=/tmp/lean-global-oracle-target/release/near-arena-oracle-v3-successor
# one run; generation.json lists all 25 runs and their arguments
NEAR_SUCCESSOR_SCENARIO=yield-congestion NEAR_XFAM_MUTATE_EVERY=4 $B gen --domain d3 --seed 102 \
  --first-chain 4 --chains 1 --blocks 260 --p-missing 0.1 --mutate-every 0 --code-mutant-p 0 \
  --ood-cap 100000 --drop-cap 12 --out yc102
python3 tools/d3/collect_xfam.py --source yc102 --out col/yc102          # fails on a violated family fact
cat col/yc102/honest.txt col/yc102/mutants.txt > all.txt
python3 tools/d3/replay_successor.py --tier allFeatures --cases-file all.txt --out rep --jobs 8   # also storageResolution
python3 tools/mainnet-replay/blind_corpus.py --binary spec/lean/v3/.lake/build/bin/nearspec-v3-check-successor \
  --cases col/yc102/honest.txt --root / --out blind
python3 tools/d3/summarize_xfam.py --col col --runs yc102 ... --out replay --rows
python3 tools/d3/xfam_tables.py col/yc10*/manifest.json --out steps/yield-congestion.md
python3 tools/d3/select_xfam_fixtures.py --collected col/yc102 ... --out cases --honest 8 --mutants-per-case 3
```

Population: a run's family population is every honest chunk with scenario activity, plus every
mutant of such a chunk. Scenario activity means the chunk includes a scenario transaction, ran
`xfam`/`xcurve`, or applies a receipt of a scenario step. For `genesis-tx` it also covers every
genesis-B2 chunk and every chunk up to height 10004. `NEAR_XFAM_MUTATE_EVERY=k` gives one in k
relevant chunks the full mutant set: header, trusted-fact, transaction-validity, witness, code-blob
and drop-node mutants. Genesis-B2 chunks always get the full set.

Lean replays curve chunks slowly: a chunk with several BLS pairings or G2 multiexps takes up to
about 2 minutes. Run cc201 therefore replays all of its 5,009 mutants. The other curve runs replay
every 16th (cc202–cc204) or 12th (cc211–cc214) mutant of the sorted list, and all of their honest
chunks. `generation.json` records the binaries, sources, contracts and the run script. A run with
the baseline sources (`HEAD`) and with this tree, both without a scenario, is **byte-identical**:
seed 7, 2 chains, 40 blocks, `--mutate-every 10`, 8,545 files, `diff -r` empty.

### Deliberate-breakage controls (`controls.json`)

Each control is a checker built from this tree with one deliberate bug. Every bug applies only to
its own family's semantics:

| control | breakage | runs | detected (rows that flip against nearcore) |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | yield-timeout loop ignores the compute limit (`StorageExecution.yieldLoop`) | yc101–105, honest + mutants | 348 / 276 / 290 / 263 / 283; 72–122 honest chunks per run rejected (`MissingTrieValue (promise yield timeout)`: nearcore never read the queue entry) |
| C2 | BLS-writer per-element charge taken from the bytes read, after the read (`blsWriter`) | cc201, cc202, honest | 45 / 45 honest chunks rejected (register input, gas exhaustion at the element charge) |
| C3 | genesis-B2 last-chunk transaction root not checked (both genesis paths) | gx501–504, honest + mutants | 15 per run: every `g.tx_in_genesis_chunk` mutant (nearcore: transaction-root mismatch) **accepted**; the `flag0` twins are still rejected by the all-1 `tx_valid` check |
| C4 | refund never credited to the signer's gas key (`actTransfer`) | gk301–304, honest + mutants | 31 / 31 / 33 / 33, including 6–8 honest chunks per run (also at zero gas price: deposit refunds of gas-key-signed calls) |
| C5 | a failed action result keeps its emitted receipts (`AR.fail`) | rb401–404, honest | 5 / 7 / 4 / 6 honest composition chunks rejected (`InvalidOutcomesProof`) |

gk303's C4 count excludes its 3 named-OOD rows, which are unchanged from the normal checker.

## 1. Yields under congestion (`yield-congestion`, W07/W09)

Shapes are 4–6 shards with a chunk gas limit of 10 Tgas (chain 3) or 60 Tgas (chains 4, 9, 14, 19).
Two floods of `burn` receipts (`sha256` on empty input; the gas is in the host base cost) run at
offsets 4–13 and 194–205: four local and one cross-shard per height. They fill the yield shard's
delayed queue around the early resumes and around the timeouts, 200 blocks after creation.
Congestion is real and comes from nearcore's own chunk headers (`manifests/yc*.json.gz` `facts`):

| run | gas limit | yield-shard headers with delayed gas > 0 | max delayed gas | headers with buffered gas > 0 (caller shard) | yield receipts applied with a non-empty delayed queue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| yc101 | 10 Tgas | 232 | 11.9 PGas | 6 (1) | 46 |
| yc102 | 60 Tgas | 108 | 6.1 PGas | 335 (60) | 37 |
| yc103 | 60 Tgas | 91 | 6.1 PGas | 88 (19) | 37 |
| yc104 | 60 Tgas | 103 | 5.4 PGas | 415 (50) | 37 |
| yc105 | 60 Tgas | 103 | 5.2 PGas | 45 (12) | 38 |

Per-step nearcore outcomes are in `steps/yield-congestion.md`. In that table, `Q` means the yield
shard's delayed queue was non-empty when the receipt was applied. `+N` is the callback's height
minus the create receipt's height. The outcomes:

- **Timeouts falling while the delayed queue is non-empty** (steps 01, 02). On the 60 Tgas chains
  the timeout callback (`02`) runs **+234 to +245** blocks after creation instead of +201: the
  timeout loop is skipped while the compute limit is exhausted (`lib.rs:3003`). On the 10 Tgas
  chain the queue never drains, so the due timeouts are still deferred when the run ends. Step 13
  (a cross-shard create during the first flood) times out at +201 to +213.
- **A resume that wins over a due but deferred timeout** (06 + 14). The resume transaction is
  applied at creation + 203, after the expiry. Its `PromiseResume` waits in the delayed queue, the
  deferred timeout does not fire first, and the callback gets the payload (`01 late-but-first`) at
  +224 to +229.
- **Resumes and creates under congestion.** These are a local resume (07, 03), a create
  delayed behind the flood (08), and the create-then-resume ordering. In one run the local resume
  receipt overtook its delayed create and returned `02`, meaning no data id stored yet (step 11).
- **A cross-shard resume trigger that is buffered and then delayed** (09 → 00). The 200 KB resume
  transaction from shard 1 exceeds the base bandwidth grant (100 KB). It is buffered on the
  caller's shard (nonzero `buffered_receipts_gas` there) and then delayed on the yield shard, and
  the callback arrives +36 to +106 blocks after creation.
- **A resume after 360 KB of own-shard receipts in the same receipt** (10 → 04): six 60 KB
  promises to self, then the resume.
- **Timeout callbacks that emit a cross-shard receipt** (02, 13: callbacks with the `0x80` slot
  bit), and a small cross-shard resume delayed at the receiver (12 → 05).

PromiseYield and PromiseResume receipts themselves never cross shards; this is pinned behaviour, as
`../yield-fixtures/README.md` explains. The "cross-shard" part is the triggering receipt, which is
buffered on the sending shard and delayed on the receiving one.

## 2. Curve hosts from callbacks and promise chains (`curve-callbacks`, W05)

`contracts`: `xcurve` (generated in `xfam.rs`) has every curve host with five input sources: call
arguments in memory, register 0, out-of-bounds memory, promise result in memory, and promise result
in register. The inputs are the first non-empty `pos`, first `neg`, first `edge` and the
gas-exhaustion input of each host from `curve_hosts::steps()`. Per input there are these variants:

- `then` callback on the arguments, on the promise result in memory, and on the promise result in a
  register;
- a three-promise chain (`then2`);
- a callback after a promise to the other shard's contract (`thenx`);
- a yield callback on its arguments, or on the resume payload in memory or in a register, resumed in
  the same receipt;
- yields resumed by a later cross-shard transaction (`yslot` + `rslot`).

There are 363 steps per run, with every third signed from another shard. Outcomes are in
`steps/curve-callbacks.md`; `retN` is the host's u64 return.

**W05 row "register/out-of-bounds input for the remaining BLS writers" is covered.** `p2_sum`,
`g1_multiexp`, `g2_multiexp`, `map_fp_to_g1`, `map_fp2_to_g2`, `p1_decompress` and `p2_decompress`
were each called with register input (`value_len = u64::MAX`) on pos/neg/edge inputs, and every
one fails with `Integer overflow` at the per-element charge. The same holds from a promise result
in a register (`_rr`). Each also gets out-of-bounds memory (`Accessed memory outside the bounds`) and
a register read of a missing promise result (`Accessed invalid promise result index: 0`). The
three alt_bn128 hosts and the BLS pairing take register input from promise results and return
normally (0/1 or their input errors), as their direct register variants already did. Gas exhaustion at the element charge also happens inside
callbacks (`Exceeded the prepaid gas`).

## 3. Transaction-bearing genesis (`genesis-tx`, W10)

**nearcore cannot produce a genesis-B2 witness whose main transition has transactions.** The
transactions applied by a witness's main transition are the last new chunk's (`state_witness
.transactions()`, whose root must equal that chunk's `tx_root`, `chunk_validation.rs:368-376`). For
a genesis B2 that chunk is the genesis chunk. `genesis_chunk` (`core/primitives/src/genesis/chunk.rs:64-95`)
builds every genesis chunk with `validated_txs = vec![]` and `tx_root = CryptoHash::default()`, which
equals `merklize([])`. The genesis branch also applies nothing (`MainTransition::Genesis`,
`chunk_validation.rs:403-413, 602`), and its `tx_valid` flags are derived all-1
(`chunk_validation.rs:380-382`).

What nearcore does produce are genesis-B2 witnesses whose own chunk carries transactions
(`new_transactions`, checked at `chunk_validation.rs:321-340` and in the header's `tx_root`). The
scenario submits twelve kinds of transactions before the first three blocks: cross-shard emits
with callbacks, emit-then-panic, yields, deposits to failing calls, sub-account creation on another
shard, deploy+call, gas-key add+fund, a state init with missing global code, cross-shard calls, and
deletion of a missing key. Missing chunks (`--p-missing 0.3`) add implicit transitions on top of the
genesis chunk extra.

Facts: across 4 seeds × 3 chains there are 60 genesis-B2 chunks. All 60 carry new transactions
and none has main-transition transactions. Their implicit-transition counts are 1 (40 chunks),
2 (10), 3 (9) and 6 (1). Each genesis-B2 chunk gets the full mutant set plus the `g.*` mutants:

| mutant | judge | nearcore / expected | Lean |
|---|---|---|---|
| `g.tx_in_genesis_chunk` (a new transaction placed in the genesis chunk's list, `tx_valid = [1]`) | nearcore (cold) | reject: transaction root | reject: transaction root of the last chunk |
| `g.tx_in_genesis_chunk.flag0` (same, `tx_valid = [0]`) | nearcore (cold) | reject | reject |
| `g.tx_valid_extra_flag1/0` (flags without transactions) | claim-v3 discipline | reject | reject: `tx_valid` length |
| `g.genesis_extra.none` | claim-v3 discipline | reject | reject: `genesis_chunk_extra` missing |
| `g.genesis_extra.{state_root,gas_limit,gas_used,balance_burnt,outcome_root,congestion}` | derived (trusted fact differs from the store) | reject | reject (post state root, `InvalidGasLimit`, …) |

This means `txValid.all` on a genesis B2 is reachable only with an empty transaction list in any
witness nearcore accepts. With a transaction present, the transaction-root check rejects first.
Control C3 shows that without it, Lean would accept the nearcore-invalid `g.tx_in_genesis_chunk`.
The all-1 check then still rejects the `flag0` twin. Header/chunk-extra checks and implicit
transitions are exercised by the full mutant set of all 60 cases. The first non-genesis chunk of
each shard applies those first transactions and is in the population.

## 4. Gas keys, target side (`gaskey-target`, W07)

`steps/gaskey-target.md` lists 30 steps, all with identical outcome classes in the 4 runs (two of
them at a nonzero gas price). Each runs on a shard other than the signer's:

- funding by `TransferToGasKey` receipts (also next to a transfer), and to a missing key or a non-gas
  key, which fails with a refund;
- nonce-index transactions (indices 0, 1, 2; two with index 0 in one chunk) whose receipts run on
  other shards, including a failing call with a 1 NEAR deposit. The deposit and gas refunds go back
  to the **gas key**, which C4 confirms;
- a refund that arrives after its gas key was deleted (it goes to the account);
- withdraw (partial, too much, all-then-delete);
- `CreateAccount` receipts that add, fund and then delete a gas key (1,000 yocto: burnt, ok; 3 NEAR:
  `GasKeyBalanceTooHigh`, whole receipt rolled back) or delete the account (1,000 yocto ok; 2 NEAR
  `GasKeyBalanceTooHigh`);
- a new account's gas key signing cross-shard transactions;
- funding a deleted gas key.

Ordinary transfers and calls share chunks with these receipts.

## 5. Rollback/refund composition (`rollback-refund`, W07/W08/W09)

There are four rounds. In each, `s1rbs` submits ten transactions at one height to targets on
`s0…`, so one chunk of that shard applies all of them as incoming receipts. The chunk contains:

- a state init with missing global code (refund);
- a state init with insufficient deposit (`LackBalanceForState`, refund);
- a state init followed by an emitting call that fails with `BalanceExceeded`, rolling back the state
  init;
- a `CreateAccount` + `UseGlobalContract` (by account or by hash) whose init call panics, rolling back
  the account and refunding the transfer;
- a local deploy whose init succeeds, and one whose init fails (deploy rolled back);
- an emitting call that panics (emitted receipts dropped, deposit refunded);
- an emitting call to a failing callee (callee failure, callback, refund of the promise deposit);
- a failing call with a deposit, and an ordinary transfer.

Two local transactions of the global-code user join the same chunk. Outcomes are in
`steps/rollback-refund.md`, identical in the 4 runs. Control C5 is detected in every composition
chunk.

## Non-matching rows

The 9 rows are code-blob mutants (`code.drop`/`flip`/`truncate`) of baseline chunks in which
`d3rich` is deployed in the chunk and also called as pre-state code:

| run | chunk | rows | nearcore |
|---|---|---|---|
| gk303 | `02-h10037-s5` | 3 | reject (MissingTrieValue) |
| cc201 | `00-h10021-s1` | 3 | drop: **accept**, flip/truncate: reject |
| cc203 | `02-h10073-s4` | 1 | reject |
| rb402 | `01-h10028-s0` (a rollback composition chunk) | 2 | drop/flip of the `d3rich` blob: **accept** |

Lean (`Successor/CodeResolution.lean` `resolveColdCode`) returns `out of domain (e.wasm-α): contract
preparation pipelined before the in-chunk deploy that serves its code`. That is the W08 named
exclusion for nearcore's preparation-pipeline thread race (`Successor/Pipeline.lean`). Lean never
accepts or rejects these. nearcore's cold verdicts on equivalent mutants of one chunk differ (cc201:
drop accepted, flip and truncate rejected), which is consistent with a timing-dependent cache hit.
The replay harness counts them as `domain_exclusion_where_in_domain` or
`domain_exclusion_unasserted`, because the generator's classifier labels these mutants
`e.code_cache`, not `e.wasm-α`.

## Not covered

- PromiseYield/PromiseResume receipts crossing shards: protocol-impossible outside resharding (W11).
- On the 10 Tgas shape, deferred timeouts never fire within the run; on the 60 Tgas shapes they do.
- `curve-callbacks` timeouts (a timed-out yield whose callback runs a host) were dropped to keep
  runs short; timeout callbacks are covered without hosts by `yield-congestion`.
- `state-init-then-emit` never reaches a successful emit: the derived account cannot pay the
  1,000-yocto promise amounts (`BalanceExceeded`), so the state init is rolled back.
- Curve mutants of cc202–cc204 and cc211–cc214 are sampled (1/16, 1/12). All their honest chunks are
  replayed.
- The later cross-shard resumes of `curve-callbacks` (steps 343–362) come at the end of the
  schedule. In the 95-block runs (cc201–cc204) most of their callbacks had not executed when the run
  stopped, so cc211–cc214 run for 115 blocks. `steps/curve-callbacks.md` marks the pending ones.
- `ML-DSA` keys, secp256k1 gas keys on the target side, and gas-key function-call permissions.
